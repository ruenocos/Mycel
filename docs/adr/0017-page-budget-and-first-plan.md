# 0017 Page budget and first plan: a year of work, existing pages first

**Status:** accepted, 15 Nov 2026 (issue #42, steps 5-7). Builds on 0014. Budget size chosen by Wons on 15 Nov ("a year of work").

**Decision:** a map proposes what a small business can do in a year, and says what to do first.

- **Every existing page gets a job first:** serves (it is a node's page), competes (it fights another page for one node), supports (a product in its shop category, an article under its topic) or outside (with a reason). A page whose words fully match a planned new page serves it: improve beats write.
- **Value, not volume, orders the work:** offering tier x buying stage (buy > compare > learn) x click price x opportunity (an existing page, more so one already seen in Google). Volume only breaks ties.
- **Page budget = a year at one page a week (52 steps).** Pages to improve and conflicts to resolve count first; new pages get the rest, split over the offerings by tier (top 3, normal 2, side 1), about 40% of each share for pages that teach. Offering pages and the pages an existing page hangs under are never cut. A page over budget becomes a section of the page above it when it is a close variant, else it goes to the backlog (`leftOut`, reason `over_budget`) with its value, so the owner can pull it back.
- **The first plan is about 10 steps**, in this order: conflicts on money pages, money pages of the top offerings to improve, missing top-offering pages, their support pages. Every step is tied to one of the (at most three) top offerings or to a conflict.
- **Names and why-texts are fact-checked:** a place, price or service must be in the business profile, praise needs a fact behind it, SEO jargon never reaches the owner. A failing text is rewritten by the model once (`map-fix`), else replaced by a plain sentence from data in the market language. Slugs come from the page name when every word of it was searched.
- **Measured by** `mycel eval map`: first-plan steps not tied, existing pages with a job, failed fact checks, slug words nobody searched, new pages.

**Why a budget and not a demand threshold:** round 8 still proposed 200-300 new pages per test client, nearly all with real search demand and an offering. A threshold barely cuts; reviewers scored "too many pages" as the main problem. A budget tied to the work also makes existing pages count: a site with more to fix gets fewer new pages.

**Not yet:** GSC positions for the quick-win weight (5-20 = quick win; today any GSC impressions count), a per-site-type budget (webshop vs local service), the owner pulling backlog pages into the plan from the explorer.
